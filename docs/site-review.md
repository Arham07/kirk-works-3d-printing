# Walking the site as a customer

Kirk asked me to go through the site the way someone who found it would, and
say what I would change. This is that pass. The three things she raised are
done; the rest is what I found on my own, ordered by how much I think each one
costs her in enquiries.

---

## The three changes she asked for

**1. The capitals are gone.** Every heading on the site now reads in sentence
case, and the brand is set the way she writes it: **KirkWorks3D**, in the
masthead, in the header and in the footer. Thirty-eight headings changed. The
only capitals left are the material names — PLA, PETG, ASA, TPU — which are
acronyms and would look wrong any other way.

Changing the case is not just a switch. A condensed typeface set in capitals
and the same face set in mixed case want different spacing, so the line spacing
and letter spacing were retuned to match, and the giant masthead word was
re-measured so it still runs edge to edge.

**2. The studio is now the first thing you see.** A photograph of the workshop —
both racing simulators, the printer rack, the shelves of filament — fills the
whole top of the page, with the masthead and the headline over it. Before this,
the first picture of anything was about 2,000 pixels down the page, which is
two and a half screens on a phone.

The studio then appears twice more in the next two screens: the desk beside
"You work directly with Kirk", and the three printers on their rack in the
Machines section.

**3. My own walkthrough** is the rest of this document.

## What I would change next

### The prices and specs looked unfinished, and now they do not

Thirty-nine places on the home page were showing a dash where a number belongs:
all four starting prices, every specification for all three printers, and the
five HueForge figures. A visitor reads that as a half-built site, not as
honesty.

That is fixed from both ends. Nothing shows a dash any more — the prices say
"Quoted per project", and the rows with no confirmed values are simply not
there. Nothing is invented.

The other end is Kirk's. **These are the numbers I need from her**, and they
are the single highest-value thing she can send:

- **The three printers.** For each one: build volume, how many nozzles, how
  many filament slots, the layer-height range, and which materials she actually
  runs on it. Straight off the machine or its spec sheet.
- **A starting price** for each of the four bands: a small custom print, a
  framed HueForge, custom design work, and a private lesson. A number she is
  happy to be held to, not an exact quote.
- **The Low N Slow HueForge**: layer count, total thickness, how many filaments,
  how many colour swaps, and the print time.
- **The six photographed pieces**: colours, print time, layer height, material.
- **Opening hours** and how far she will travel.

### Nothing on the site is a customer's job

All six pieces in the portfolio are labelled "Studio piece", which is accurate —
every one is something Kirk made for herself. But a visitor reads six pieces and
no commissions and draws the obvious conclusion.

Two or three real jobs, with the customer's permission, would do more for
enquiries than any design change on this list. Even one, with a sentence about
what the person asked for and what they got, changes the page from a portfolio
into evidence.

### The quote form does not go anywhere yet

Right now, submitting the form composes a message and hands it to whatever mail
or texting app the visitor has. On a phone that mostly works. On a desktop with
no mail client set up, it is a dead end, and the enquiry is lost without either
side knowing.

Switching it to a real form that lands in Kirk's inbox is a one-line change on
my side. It needs one decision from her: where those messages should go.

### There are no reviews anywhere

For a local business people find by scanning a card, a few named quotes are the
cheapest trust available. Three or four sentences from past customers, with
first names and what they had made, would sit naturally under the portfolio.

### The home page is long

About twenty-seven phone screens. The content is good and none of it is filler,
but the printer comparison and the materials guide are reference material —
they are what someone reads when they are already interested. Moving them to
their own pages would shorten the path from landing to phone call, and would
give both topics a page that can rank on its own.

---

## What I would not touch

Three things on this site are better than what her competitors publish, and
they should survive any future redesign.

**"See it before you pay the balance"** as a named step in the process. Nobody
else says this. It answers the fear that stops people commissioning custom work
from a stranger.

**The list of what she will not take on** — firearm parts, copies of other
people's products, anything load-bearing where failure hurts someone. Publishing
limits reads as expertise, and it filters out the enquiries that would waste her
evening.

**The FAQ answers on cost and lead time.** Both refuse to give a fake number and
explain the real variables instead. That is the voice the whole site should keep.
